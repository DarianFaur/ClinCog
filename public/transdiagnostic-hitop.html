Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology (HiTOP)
A dimensional classification built from the observed covariation of symptoms, rather than from committee-defined categories

HiTOP is a consortium of clinical researchers building a classification of mental illness from quantitative evidence about how symptoms actually co-occur. Its starting point is a list of problems with traditional nosologies that its inaugural paper sets out directly: “arbitrary boundaries between psychopathology and normality, often unclear boundaries between disorders, frequent disorder co-occurrence, heterogeneity within disorders, and diagnostic instability” (Kotov et al., 2017). Instead of assigning a person to a category, HiTOP places them on continua, and organises those continua into a hierarchy that runs from single symptoms up to a general factor shared by all mental disorders.

The model
HiTOP glossary

Each definition below is quoted from the inaugural paper (Kotov et al., 2017), reading from the bottom of the hierarchy upwards.

LevelDefinition
Homogeneous components“constellations of closely related symptom manifestations; for example, fears of working, reading, eating, or drinking in front of others form performance anxiety.”
Maladaptive traits“specific pathological personality characteristics, such as submissiveness.”
Syndromes“composites of related components/traits, such as a social anxiety syndrome that encompasses both performance anxiety and interaction anxiety.”
Subfactors“groups of closely related syndromes, such as the fear subfactor formed by strong links between social anxiety, agoraphobia, and specific phobia.”
Spectra“larger constellations of syndromes, such as an internalizing spectrum composed of syndromes from fear, distress, eating pathology, and sexual problems subfactors.”
Superspectra“extremely broad dimensions comprised of multiple spectra, such as a general factor of psychopathology that represents the liability shared by all mental disorders.”
A caution the authors state themselves: HiTOP “remains a work in progress”, and the paper describes the system as “a set of testable hypotheses that would encourage exploration rather than constrain it”, adding that the consortium seeks “to avoid reification of the system” (Kotov et al., 2017). Where this application uses HiTOP-SR scales, it uses them as severity dimensions for teaching, not as a diagnosis.
