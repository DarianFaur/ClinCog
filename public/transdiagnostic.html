The cases in this application are worked with ICD-11, which sorts people into categories. That is one answer to the question of how mental illness should be classified, and it is contested. These pages cover three alternatives the field is actively building – one that replaces categories with dimensions, one that sets classification aside to study mechanisms, and one that asks where a person is along a course rather than which box they belong in. None of them replaces ICD-11 in clinical practice today; knowing why is part of learning to use any of them.

Dimensional

HiTOP

A hierarchy built from how symptoms actually co-occur: single symptoms at the bottom, spectra such as internalizing and thought disorder in the middle, a general factor of psychopathology at the top.
Mechanistic

RDoC

NIMH's research framework: six domains of functioning crossed with units of analysis from genes to self-report. Deliberately not a diagnostic system, and its authors say so in the founding paper.
Longitudinal

Clinical staging

Borrowed from oncology: the same diagnosis early and late is not the same clinical problem. Stage 0 to 4, from at-risk to treatment resistance, used to match intervention intensity to where someone actually is.

These three are not competitors with a winner. HiTOP reorganises what we measure, RDoC reorganises what we study, and staging reorganises when we act – and the same authors often appear in more than one literature. A useful way in is Eaton et al. (2023), which reviews dimensional models, network approaches and staging side by side.
