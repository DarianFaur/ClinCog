Programming a cognitive task yourself
Open platforms for building your own reaction-time tasks and questionnaires

The batteries documented in this section are commercial or licence-restricted, and the versions inside ClinCog are open re-implementations written for teaching. If you want to build a task of your own – for a class demonstration, a bachelor’s project or a study – you do not need either. Reaction-time paradigms of exactly this kind can be written, run and analysed on free, open platforms.

PsyToolkit is the one most students start with. It is free, runs in the browser, and ships with a library of ready-made questionnaires and reaction-time experiments – including dot-probe, stop-signal and trail-making style tasks – that you can run as they are, read the code of, or modify into your own design. Its documentation is written for people who have not programmed before, which is why it works as a teaching tool rather than only a research one.

A word on responsibility: writing a task is the easy part. Deciding what a score means, whether a single session can support that meaning, and what you are entitled to say about a person on the strength of it – that is the part this course is about, and it does not change with the platform.

PsyToolkit asks that both papers be cited in any work that uses it:
Stoet, G. (2010). PsyToolkit – A software package for programming psychological experiments using Linux. Behavior Research Methods, 42(4), 1096–1104. https://doi.org/10.3758/BRM.42.4.1096
Stoet, G. (2017). PsyToolkit: A novel web-based method for running online questionnaires and reaction-time experiments. Teaching of Psychology, 44(1), 24–31. https://doi.org/10.1177/0098628316677643
