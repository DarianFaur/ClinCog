Every case closes with a cognitive task. Each page below covers one of them: what it asks the person to do, what the score is meant to represent, the empirical literature behind it, and the official source to go to next. These are starting points for your own reading, not substitutes for the primary sources cited on each page.

Batteries

Standardised test batteries

Commercial, licence-restricted instruments. The versions inside ClinCog are open re-implementations written for teaching, not the batteries themselves.

Dennis · Psychosis

MATRICS Consensus Cognitive Battery

The NIMH consensus battery for measuring cognitive change in schizophrenia: seven domains, ten tests. Dennis's case uses Trail Making A and Letter–Number Span.
Darren · Depression

CANTAB

Cambridge Neuropsychological Test Automated Battery. Darren's case uses Spatial Working Memory and One Touch Stockings of Cambridge, with the evidence on which deficits persist into remission.
Paradigms

Individual experimental paradigms

Single tasks from the experimental literature rather than commercial batteries, each built natively into the case it belongs to.

Alex · Anxiety

Dot-probe task

A reaction-time measure of attentional bias towards threat: the probe replaces either the threatening or the neutral word, and the difference in response speed is the index.
Jordan · Substance use

Stop-signal task

A measure of response inhibition. SSRT estimates how long it takes to cancel an action already under way – with an honest account of how inconsistent the substance-use findings actually are.
Jordan · Substance use

Alcohol Purchase Task

A behavioural-economics measure: how much a person says they would drink across a rising price ladder, summarised as intensity, breakpoint, Omax, Pmax and elasticity.
