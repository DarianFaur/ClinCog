Clinical Vignette

Read the case carefully before administering the assessment tools.

Jordan M., 25-year-old male, Software Engineer
Self-referred after a performance review warning
Presentation
Jordan is a 25-year-old software engineer at a mid-size tech firm. He was referred following a formal performance review in which his manager noted missed sprint deadlines, late arrivals, and a visible decline in code quality over the past six months. Jordan presents as intelligent and self-aware but minimises clinical concern: "I work in a high-stress environment , everyone on my team drinks at hackathons and after deploys. I just need to dial it back a little." He rates his motivation to change at 5/10.
Alcohol Use History
Jordan began drinking at university (age 18). Use escalated during his first engineering job (age 20–22) when he drank to manage social anxiety at networking events. Over the past 14 months use has become daily. He currently drinks 4–5 cans of beer or equivalent on weeknights and 10–14 standard drinks on weekend days, starting within an hour of finishing work from home. He has attempted to stop three times in the past year , triggered by a partner confrontation, a health scare (abnormal liver panel), and a bet with a colleague, none lasting beyond five days. On each occasion he returned to his previous intake within a week, often exceeding it.
On most occasions Jordan intends to stop after two or three drinks. He consistently reports drinking until he falls asleep at his desk or the supply runs out. He keeps a small cooler under his standing desk "so he doesn't have to keep getting up."
Withdrawal & Physical Symptoms
On abstinent days Jordan reports fine hand tremor, sweating, and severe insomnia, all resolving within 20–30 minutes of his first drink. He opens a beer before his first standup call on approximately 3–4 mornings per week. He has noticed that it now takes roughly twice as many drinks to achieve the relaxation effect he experienced 18 months ago. He denies prior seizures but acknowledges his hands shake visibly on camera during morning video calls on days he has not yet drunk.
Occupational & Social Functioning
Jordan has missed four sprint deadlines in the past quarter and received one formal written warning. He estimates spending 1–2 hours per workday anticipating his first post-work drink and managing the physical effects of the previous night's drinking. He has withdrawn from his climbing gym (previously 3×/week) and declined three team social events over the past four months. His partner of three years has issued an ultimatum and is currently sleeping in the spare room. Jordan has driven to the supermarket to restock alcohol on several occasions while over the legal limit, rationalising that "it was only five minutes down the road."
Psychiatric & Risk History
Jordan endorses frequent, intrusive urges to drink throughout the workday, particularly during debugging sessions and before code reviews. He reports irritability, middle-of-night waking, and difficulty concentrating , all worse on low-intake days. He denies a primary mood disorder when not drinking. No prior psychiatric treatment. His father was a heavy drinker (never formally assessed). Jordan is aware of his liver results and their likely cause but has not disclosed this to his GP since the initial panel.
