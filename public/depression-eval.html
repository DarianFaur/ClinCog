Clinical Vignette

Read the case carefully before administering the assessment tools.

Darren, 34
High school English teacher · First psychiatric contact
Presentation

Darren, a 34-year-old high school English teacher, has been struggling to get out of bed most mornings. Over the past few weeks, he stares at the ceiling after the alarm rings — numb — often lying there for hours before dragging himself to the couch. Once known for his energetic lectures and quick wit, he now dreads the thought of facing his students. "What's the point?" he mutters. "They're not learning anything from me anyway. Nothing I do matters."

Mood & Affective Symptoms

His colleagues have grown concerned, though he rarely responds to their messages anymore. He tells them he's overwhelmed with grading, but even small interactions feel like too much. Some days, a colleague's offhand comment in the hallway is enough to make his chest tighten with irritation — a reaction that surprises even him, since he'd never been quick to anger before. Other times, he feels nothing at all. His moods shift without warning: some mornings there is a fleeting hour of restless agitation before the flatness returns.

Cognitive & Functional Changes

He's stopped cooking, skipping meals entirely or eating whatever requires no decision. His clothes hang looser now, though he hasn't noticed. When he tries to plan even a simple task — what to eat, what to say to his department head — his mind goes blank, the thoughts sliding away before he can hold onto them. He described it recently as trying to think through fog.

Sleep & Risk History

When Darren does sleep, it's restless, full of strange half-waking dreams. On other nights he lies awake for hours, his mind circling through failures both real and imagined, unable to find a way out of the loop. He wakes unrefreshed every morning. Lately he's been thinking more and more about how everyone around him would simply be better off if he disappeared. The thoughts come and go, but they are starting to stay longer. Last week, for the first time, he found himself considering how he might act on them — a thought that frightened him enough to stop, but not enough to tell anyone. On one occasion, he pressed his nails hard into his forearm, not quite understanding why, only that the sharpness made the internal noise quieter for a moment.

Presenting Crisis

At school, he has missed several grading deadlines and once walked out of class mid-lesson, overwhelmed and on the verge of tears. Since then he has been calling in sick, unable to face another day.

His department head tried to check in afterward, but Darren could only apologize quietly, his gaze fixed on the floor.

Unlike DSM-5, ICD-11 does not require a fixed symptom count. A depressive episode requires at least one of the two core symptoms (a–b), occurring most of the day nearly every day for at least two weeks, accompanied by several of the symptoms below — the overall clinical picture, not a checklist total, determines the diagnosis. Use the live count to inform your own judgment on the Conclusion below.
